Community · Solo project

Cardiff Community Meals

Cardiff has neighbours who need a hot meal — recently discharged from hospital, living with a disability, or just going through it — and a city full of people and local kitchens who'd help if the ask was easy and trustworthy. I designed and built a bilingual (Cymraeg-first) prototype end to end: UX planning, information architecture, safeguarding flows, and the full front-end, solo.

This is a working front-end prototype — localStorage stands in for a real database and payments are simulated, by design, so the flows can be demoed and pitched before investing in a backend. The numbers you see in the screenshots are sample data, not live totals.

Role
Lead developer & UX designer
Scope
10 pages, bilingual EN/CY
Stack
HTML/CSS/JS, Bootstrap 5
Status
Phase 0 prototype, live on GitHub Pages

What it does

Safeguarding-first requestsEvery meal request is reviewed by an admin before it's ever made public — nobody vulnerable gets listed without consent.
Donate & BookDonors browse verified, anonymised requests (first name + area only) and fund a specific meal or meal-week.
Public impact dashboardRunning totals and a top-contributing-business leaderboard, so donors and partners can see the effect in real time.
Business partner tiersLocal cafés and kitchens get a public profile and Bronze/Silver/Gold recognition as they deliver more meals.
Welsh-first bilingual UXA full EN/CY toggle across every page — this is a Cardiff service, and it leads in Cymraeg by default.
Cook & Book rotasAccess-code gated private cooking rotas for closed groups, like a meal train for a new baby.

A look inside

Built to grow up

The prototype is deliberately backend-free so it can be pitched fast — but I mapped the production path too: Django/Node + PostgreSQL, Stripe Connect for escrow-style payments held until delivery, Twilio/Postmark for delivery confirmations, and a phased rollout from single-partner pilot to city-wide. Safeguarding and UK GDPR considerations were designed in from page one, not bolted on after.

Personal · Solo prototype

CarbMate

Not a medical device. Not validated. Do not use this to make real insulin decisions. An app that advises insulin doses is a regulated medical device in the UK, EU and US — this is a prototype, built and shared as one.

I'm a type 1 diabetic with disabilities, and I've always found carb counting hard — the apps that exist are either fiddly, expensive, or both. So I built the tool I wanted: type a meal in plain English, search a food database, or photograph the plate, and get a bolus suggestion with the working shown, free for anyone else who might find it useful too.

Role
Solo developer
Stack
FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, PostgreSQL
Testing
pytest — dose engine & parser
Status
Local prototype, tested; not currently deployed

What it does

Three ways in, one path outPlain English, food-database search, or a meal photo — all three converge on the same editable item list before anything is calculated.
The dose engine is isolatedapp/dose.py has no database access, no HTTP, no side effects — everything arrives as arguments, so it's testable to the standard a real certification would demand.
The AI never invents a carb valueThe language model only parses text into {food, quantity, unit} — carbohydrate figures always come from the food table, so a hallucinated number can't reach a dose.
Every dose is auditableEach log entry stores its full input snapshot and the algorithm version, so any past recommendation can be reconstructed later.
Photo estimates are drafts, not answersVision-based meal estimates always carry a confidence band and are never allowed to flow straight into a dose.
UK-oriented food data~130 foods with named portions to start, built to grow toward CoFID and Open Food Facts.
